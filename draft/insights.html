Article overview

Silent bone loss after menopause

Bone loss can happen without obvious symptoms. After menopause, lower estrogen levels can accelerate bone loss; osteoporosis may only become apparent when a fracture occurs.

Bone health is worth discussing before a problem develops. Personal and family history, previous fractures and other risk factors help guide whether a bone density test is appropriate. Regular weight-bearing and resistance exercise, adequate calcium and vitamin D, and avoiding smoking are important parts of protecting bone health.

Further reading: NIAMS · Osteoporosis

Article overview

Fertility after age 35

Fertility changes with age, but no single age or test tells the whole story for an individual. Understanding your menstrual cycle, medical history and plans can help you approach pregnancy with clearer expectations.

A preconception discussion can cover existing health conditions, medications and practical preparation for pregnancy. If pregnancy is taking longer than expected, the timing of a fertility assessment depends on age and medical history; known concerns may warrant earlier evaluation.

Further reading: ASRM · Fertility evaluation

Article overview

After 50, protein matters more than you think

Maintaining muscle matters as we get older. Protein contributes to a balanced diet that supports strength and everyday activity, alongside regular physical activity.

Fish, eggs, dairy, lean meat, beans and soy foods offer different ways to include protein in everyday meals. A varied diet is more useful than focusing on a single food or supplement. Individual needs can differ, particularly for people with kidney disease or other medical conditions; dietary changes should take those needs into account.

Further reading: NIA · Healthy meal planning
